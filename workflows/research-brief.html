Research / UNTESTED STARTER WORKFLOW

Turn a question into a research brief you can verify.

A bounded, traceable research workflow: define the decision, gather source evidence, and keep uncertainty visible.

Draft starter workflow · Untested · Design preview for editorial review, not a validated case study or report of measured results.

What you are making

A one-page brief that separates direct source facts from your interpretation. Anchor the work to one concrete decision rather than a broad topic: asking ‘Which onboarding step should I inspect first?’ sets a clearer boundary than ‘Research onboarding.’ This is an untested starter workflow for design preview, not a case study or a report of measured results.

01 / Define the decision boundary

Write down the question, intended reader, relevant geography, date range, and what would change your decision. Gather a tight set of primary sources: official documentation, original research, or first-party announcements. A vendor page can confirm what features a company states it offers, but it cannot independently establish that its product is best.

If your AI tool cannot browse live pages, paste the source text yourself. Including a URL in a prompt is not proof the tool opened it. Remove confidential customer or business data before using any tool not approved for that information.

02 / Build an evidence table before drafting

Ask for a table with one row per key claim: claim, source URL, publication or update date, supporting passage, and source limitation. Require the model to mark inaccessible pages and unsupported assertions explicitly instead of filling gaps. Review this table before asking for the brief.

OpenAI’s search guidance notes that citations can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect. Open each important link and check the underlying passage yourself; a formatted citation in an AI response is a pointer to check, not completed verification.

03 / Draft around the decision and its gaps

Use four sections: short answer, evidence, uncertainties, and next action. Label any synthesis or inference clearly as an interpretation. When sources conflict, keep that disagreement visible rather than asking the model to smooth it away. If the evidence does not answer the question, state that gap as the brief’s answer.

Before you use it

Check names, dates, units, and whether each cited page supports the exact wording in your draft. Cut claims that survive only because they sound plausible. Save the brief with its question, sources, and review date so you can revisit it when facts change. This workflow does not replace specialist review for high-stakes decisions.

Copyable starter prompt

Replace the bracketed fields below, and review what you share before pasting into an AI tool.

Help me prepare a traceable research brief for this decision: [decision]. Reader: [reader]. Scope and date range: [scope]. Use only sources you can directly access or text I provide. First, produce an evidence table with one row per claim: claim, URL, source date, supporting passage, and limitation. Mark inaccessible or missing evidence explicitly instead of guessing. Separate source facts from your interpretation. Second, using only that table, draft a one-page brief with: 1) Short answer, 2) Evidence, 3) Uncertainties, and 4) One next action. Do not invent references, smooth over conflicting sources, or treat a vendor claim as independent validation. Sources or source text: [paste].
