Operations · STARTER WORKFLOW

Turn raw weekly notes into a clear Monday handoff.

Organize scattered work notes into a structured summary of completed work, active blockers, and proposed next priorities.

Proposed starter workflow · Untested · Preview for editorial review rather than a validated case study.

What this builds

You will create a concise weekly handoff document for your own planning rather than an automated status report. It separates finished work, open questions, and a short list of candidate priorities for next week. This proposed starter workflow is untested; you gather the raw notes and approve the final plan yourself.

01 / Gather focused, redacted notes

Compile a single dated document with last week’s targets, finished deliverables, carry-over tasks, known deadlines, and key decisions. Include links to the actual artifacts for your own reference. Strip out client names, credentials, and private business details before pasting the text into an AI tool. Avoid hooking up a live inbox or customer database just to draft a weekly summary.

Anthropic’s guidance on context engineering emphasizes feeding models focused, well-structured inputs rather than dumping an entire raw history, and highlights the value of keeping compact structured notes across sessions. The manual workflow below is our own proposed adaptation of those principles, not a tested system from Anthropic.

02 / Separate verified progress from assumptions

Instruct the model to sort your notes into four buckets: completed work backed by evidence, open tasks, blocked items with their specific dependency, and unclear or conflicting notes. Mentioning a task in a meeting note does not mean it shipped. Tell the model to flag contradictions as open questions rather than guessing, and to separate hard commitments from its own suggested priorities.

03 / Draft a realistic shortlist

State your realistic working hours and fixed calendar commitments for the coming week. Have the model propose at most three priorities, pairing each with an immediate next step and a clear definition of done. Weigh the trade-offs yourself: an AI draft only knows the constraints you typed in, and it should never quietly convert a rough estimate into a hard deadline.

Review and archive

Edit the draft for accuracy and save a dated copy you actually trust. When next week arrives, carry forward only the unfinished items and lasting decisions while keeping your raw notes on hand to verify details. This manual process never edits your calendar, messages teammates, or updates task trackers.

If you use this format over several weeks, compare your saved handoffs against what actually happened. Look for recurring blind spots—like overlooked dependencies, stale carry-over tasks, or assumed completions—and tighten your note-taking template before considering any automation.

Starter prompt

Fill in the bracketed placeholders and remove any sensitive details before pasting into an AI tool.

Build a weekly review using only the notes provided below. Sort items into four sections: (1) completed work with supporting evidence from the notes, (2) open work, (3) blocked work with its stated dependency, and (4) unknowns or contradictions. Do not assume a task is finished just because it was discussed. Based on my available capacity of [capacity] and fixed commitments of [commitments], propose up to three priorities for next week, each with a concrete next action and a definition of done. Clearly label your suggestions as proposals, keep explicit deadlines intact, and do not invent task owners or commitments. Close with a list of open questions I need to resolve. Do not send messages or modify any external tools. Notes: [paste dated notes].
